## Verdict against the pre-registered reading

**Research R7, case 1, every run completed.** The policy drives this distribution of tracks, not only the ten it was reported on. M3's recorded limit closes.

**The intervals are the point.** Both sets read 100 per cent, and a bare percentage would
say they are the same result. They are not: ten perfect runs are consistent with a policy
that fails one track in four ([72.2, 100.0]), while thirty-three are not ([89.6, 100.0]). 
What this sweep bought is a narrower bound, not a higher number.

## What this cannot say

- **The new tracks come from the same generator under the same acceptance bound**, so the
  claim is about a distribution of tracks rather than about track design in general. A
  different generator, a different topology or a real road is a larger question.
- **They are slightly easier, not harder**, and that was measured before the sweep rather
  than discovered after: pooled peak steering demand 0.695 against training's 0.789 and
  held-out's 0.738. So this is not a claim that the policy handles harder tracks.
- **It says nothing new about M3.** M3 was closed on the evidence it named. This is a
  measurement recorded beside that verdict, and it would have been recorded the same way
  if it had come out badly.
